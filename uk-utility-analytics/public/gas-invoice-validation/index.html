Gas bill validation

Gas invoice validation

Gas invoices add a step that electricity bills do not: the metered volume has to be converted into the kilowatt hours you are charged for. We check that conversion, the meter details, the rates and the taxes, and report any discrepancy with the evidence behind it.

How a gas meter reading becomes a charge

Your meter records volume. Your invoice charges energy. The conversion uses a standard correction factor and the calorific value of the gas for the billing period.

Imperial meters record hundreds of cubic feet, which are converted to cubic metres first. If a metric meter is treated as imperial, or the other way round, every charge that follows is wrong. That is one of the first things we check.

What we check on a gas invoice

MPRN and meter details

The Meter Point Reference Number, meter serial number and site details match your records. See MPAN & MPRN checks.

Meter readings

Opening and closing reads follow on from the previous invoice, and estimated readings are identified.

Units and conversion

Imperial or metric units, the correction factor and the calorific value used to calculate kWh.

Annual Quantity (AQ)

Where consumption is estimated, the AQ used is reviewed against your actual usage pattern.

Unit rates and standing charges

Compared with the contract or price confirmation. See contract rate verification.

Climate Change Levy and VAT

CCL applied correctly, any relief or exemption reflected, and the VAT rate and totals checked.

Common issues we look for

  • Metric and imperial units confused in the conversion
  • Readings that do not follow on from the previous invoice
  • Long runs of estimated readings based on an outdated AQ
  • Rates or standing charges that differ from the contract
  • Supplies continuing at out-of-contract rates after a renewal

What we need from you

  • The gas invoices to be checked
  • The contract or price confirmation for each MPRN
  • Meter readings or photos of the meter, where available
  • Any CCL or VAT certificates relevant to the sites
FAQ

Gas invoice validation questions

Why does a gas invoice show cubic metres and kWh?

The meter measures the volume of gas, but suppliers charge for energy. The volume is converted to kWh using a correction factor and the calorific value, as shown above.

Do you check imperial gas meters?

Yes. We check that the meter type on the invoice matches the meter on site and that the right unit conversion has been applied.

What is an AQ?

The Annual Quantity is the expected yearly gas consumption for a supply point. It is used to estimate bills when actual readings are not available, so an outdated AQ can lead to inaccurate estimated invoices.
