What an MPAN tells you

The MPAN on an electricity invoice is usually shown in a box beginning with an S. The bottom line is the unique 13-digit core number for the supply. The top line describes how the supply is set up for billing.

An MPRN is the gas equivalent: a single reference number, usually between 6 and 10 digits, that identifies the gas supply point.

What we check

MPAN and MPRN matching

The supply numbers on each invoice match your site list and contract schedule, with no supplies missing or billed twice.

Meter serial numbers

The meter billed is the meter on site, including after a meter exchange.

Meter reading continuity

Each opening reading follows on from the previous closing reading, with no gaps, overlaps or reversals.

Actual and estimated reads

Estimated readings identified, with long runs of estimates and catch-up bills flagged.

Consumption analysis

Billed consumption calculated from the readings, and compared with previous periods for unexplained changes.

EAC and AQ review

The Estimated Annual Consumption (electricity) or Annual Quantity (gas) used for estimates, compared with actual usage.

Common issues we look for

  • An invoice for an MPAN or MPRN that does not belong to the site
  • Readings that do not follow on after a meter exchange
  • An EAC or AQ far from actual usage, leading to inaccurate estimates
  • Consumption that changes sharply with no explanation

FAQ

MPAN and MPRN questions

Where do I find my MPAN?

It is printed on your electricity invoice, usually in a box beginning with the letter S. Some invoices show only the 13-digit bottom line.

Where do I find my MPRN?

It is printed on your gas invoice, often labelled as the meter point reference or supply point number.

What is an EAC?

The Estimated Annual Consumption is the expected yearly electricity use for a supply. It is used to estimate consumption when no actual reading is available, so an inaccurate EAC can produce inaccurate estimated invoices.
